# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Yohannes's behavioral profile identifies them as a **Builder-Learner** pattern. A hands-on engineer who thrives on solving complex problems with real user impact, while continuously learning and sharing knowledge with peers. Prefers small, communicative teams where ownership and direct feedback are the norm.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Achievement | High | Drives toward shipping complete, working solutions. Capstone project built across 4 tech stacks end-to-end. |
| Learning | High | Self-directed learner. Completed A2SV program alongside work, continuously picks up new technologies. |
| Autonomy | Medium-High | Prefers owning features end-to-end but values collaborative architecture decisions. |
| Social | Medium | Enjoys knowledge-sharing and tech discussions, but prefers small teams over large social environments. |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Hands-on builder:** Consistently takes projects from concept to deployment across the full stack. The capstone project demonstrates ability to architect and ship complex distributed systems independently.
- **Cross-functional versatility:** Moves between AI/ML, backend, and full-stack work comfortably. Not siloed into one domain.
- **Self-directed growth:** Pursued A2SV's intensive program while working, showing commitment to skill development beyond requirements.

## How You Work Best
- Small teams (3-5 engineers) with clear communication and short sprint cycles
- Environments that allow learning and teaching, where skill growth is valued
- Projects with tangible user impact and complex technical challenges
- Positive communication: giving and receiving direct feedback
- Ownership of features with room for creativity

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Early career:** Frame as "fresh perspective combined with production experience across multiple stacks" rather than limited years of experience.
- ** breadth vs depth:** Frame versatile full-stack + AI background as "ability to bridge technical domains" rather than lack of deep specialization.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "small team" / "autonomous" / "ownership"
- "complex problems" / "challenging projects"
- "learning culture" / "growth mindset"
- "ship quickly" / "fast-paced"
- "impact on users"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "large cross-functional teams" (10+ engineers)
- "maintenance-focused role" / "legacy systems"
- "strict hierarchical process" / "heavy documentation requirements"
- "on-call rotation" / "24/7 support"

## Management Style Preferences
- Prefers mentors/peers who give direct, honest feedback
- Likes to ask questions, learn from others, and contribute ideas
- Enjoys discussing new tech and integration opportunities
- Dislikes micromanagement and unnecessary process overhead

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasize ownership, shipping, and impact. Mention specific projects delivered end-to-end.
- **CV:** Highlight cross-functional work and production deployments. Show breadth as a strength.
- **Interviews:** Use STAR examples showing complex problem-solving and delivery in small-team settings.
- **Don't overstate:** Don't claim senior-level leadership or team management experience. Position as a high-impact individual contributor who grows into tech lead roles naturally.
